Information Guide

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions regarding recruitment, departmental operations, field protocols, and dispatch procedures.

Q1: How do I join the San Andreas Department of Transportation?

Applicants must hold a valid driver's license, clear a background check, maintain an active rank of Probationary Civilian or higher in the community network, and submit an application via our official recruitment form linked in the navigation bar.

Q2: What are our official headquarters and maintenance facility locations?

SADOT operates two primary regional hubs across the state: our urban facility in Los Santos located at 796 Popular Street, and our northern county depot located at 228 Joshua Road in Blaine County.

Q3: How do citizens or allied departments request SADOT assistance in-game?

To contact SADOT while in-game, please use the /services chat and/or join the primary SADOT radio frequency. Callers must provide an in-depth description of the call type and the exact location.

Q4: Do SADOT operators have law enforcement or police powers?

No. SADOT is strictly a civilian public works and infrastructure agency. Operators possess zero peace officer authority, cannot conduct traffic stops or vehicle pursuits, and cannot perform arrests or criminal investigations.

Q5: What is the monthly activity requirement for rostered personnel?

All active roster personnel must log a minimum of three (3) operational hours per month directly recorded within the departmental patrol logs to maintain active employment standing and vehicle privileges.

Q6: How long does the Probationary Operator (Cadet) period last?

The standard probationary period spans seven (7) to fourteen (14) continuous calendar days. During this time, cadets must complete all four practical evaluations under the direct supervision of a senior operator or supervisor.

Q7: Do I need special qualifications to operate heavy wreckers or bulldozers?

Yes. Operating heavy machinery (such as commercial lowboys, heavy wreckers, and bulldozers) is strictly restricted to personnel holding a verified Heavy Duty (HVY) Certification or Supervisory rank.

Q8: How does SADOT coordinate with police and fire departments during major incidents?

SADOT units operate under Unified Command on major scenes. Arriving operators must stage defensively, establish contact with the on-scene LEO or Fire/EMS Scene Commander, and follow their directives until roadway control is transferred.

Q9: What radio communication standards are expected on duty?

Operators must maintain professional plain-language communication discipline or standard operational code syntax. Transmissions must always include your unique agency callsign (e.g., DOT-100 series) and clear status updates.

Q10: How can citizens submit compliments or feedback regarding field operations?

Citizens or allied agency members can submit official feedback, commendations, or administrative inquiries through our community Discord channels or by reaching out to the Executive Command Staff via the department portal.
